1. Dataset and tools

I used the Kaggle mirror of the CASAS Smart Home dataset (Zenodo 15708568), home hh101, which is a single resident with six room-level ambient sensors (PIR motion ON/OFF and door OPEN/CLOSE). It contains two files:

  • a raw stream of 1,286,244 unlabelled events (2012-07-18 to 2013-07-25);
  • a labelled subset of 222,858 events (2012-07-20 to 2012-09-17), where activities are marked as Activity="begin" … Activity="end".

The work was done in Jupyter/Kaggle notebooks with pandas (vectorised parsing, categorical dtypes: the one-year stream takes 13 MB in memory), matplotlib/seaborn for visualisation and scikit-learn for modelling. The begin/end annotations were expanded to one label per event (events outside any segment are labelled Other). Each annotated segment was then collapsed into an activity episode, giving 2,677 episodes over 34 activity labels.

2. Exploratory data analysis (Task 1, 01_EDA_CASAS.ipynb)

  • Quality: there are no missing values and no duplicate rows. The labelled file has one out-of-order timestamp, which was fixed by sorting. The only outage is a two-day gap in July 2013. The home averages about 3,500 events per day.
  • Sensors: motion events make up 99.4% of the data. The living room (33%), bedroom (23%) and kitchen (22%) dominate, and a typical motion trigger lasts 1–2 s. Inter-event times are heavy-tailed (median 1.2 s, 99th percentile 341 s), so the stream is bursty.
  • Circadian rhythm: activity peaks at 09–12h, and only 8.6% of events occur between 00:00 and 06:00. Kitchen activity peaks at 08–10h, and weekends have about 10% more events than weekdays.
  • Activities: 27.9% of labelled-file events are Other. The labels are highly imbalanced: five activities (Toilet, Watch_TV, Sleep_Out_Of_Bed, Personal_Hygiene, Dress) make up 46% of episodes, and nine labels have fewer than 20 episodes. Durations range from seconds (Wake_Up) to about 5 h (Sleep). Each activity has a distinct time and room signature, e.g. Morning_Meds at 06–08h, Cook/Eat_Dinner at 17–19h and Bed_Toilet_Transition at 02–05h.
  • Transitions: many transitions are almost deterministic (Go_To_Sleep→Sleep 1.00, Leave_Home→Enter_Home 0.99, Bathe→Dress 0.95). The entropy of the next activity falls from 4.42 bits to 2.64 bits given the current activity, and to 2.02 / 1.79 bits when the time of day / the previous activity is also known. This directly motivates the model design.

3. Next-activity temporal model (Task 2, 02_Next_Activity_Model.ipynb)

Formulation. When episode t ends, predict the activity of episode t+1. Nine labels with fewer than 20 episodes are merged into a Rare class, which leaves 26 classes. For each episode the features are:

  • the current activity and the three previous activities (lags);
  • the end time as cyclic sin/cos(hour), plus day of week and a weekend flag;
  • log duration and log event count of the current episode;
  • the last sensor (room) that fired;
  • the idle time since the previous episode.

All features are available at prediction time. The data is split chronologically: 70% train (Jul 20–Sep 1), 15% validation (Sep 1–9) and 15% test (Sep 9–17, 402 transitions).

Models.

  • Baselines: majority class, and Markov chains P(next | current), P(next | previous, current) and the time-aware P(next | current, 3-hour bin), each with add-α smoothing and back-off.
  • Main model: HistGradientBoostingClassifier with native categorical features. Three configurations were compared on validation, and the best was refit on train+validation.

Analysis. Each source of temporal information improves on the one before: sequence history (Markov-2), time of day (time-aware Markov), and their combination with episode context (gradient boosting). Gradient boosting raises accuracy from 0.34 to 0.46 and macro-F1 from 0.26 to 0.47 over the first-order chain.

  • Feature importance: by permutation importance, the most useful features are the current activity and the time of day, followed by earlier lags.
  • What works: routine transitions are predicted well (Enter_Home F1 1.00; Read, Eat_Breakfast, Sleep and Wake_Up F1 ≥ 0.82), and accuracy is highest in the structured morning (72% at 06–09h).
  • What fails: most errors involve short "filler" activities that can follow almost anything (Toilet, Phone, Drink, Personal_Hygiene) and the heterogeneous Rare class.

4. Conclusion

The pipeline runs end to end: raw sensor text, then label expansion, episodes, leakage-free temporal features, and chronological evaluation. The resulting model predicts the next activity with 46% top-1 and 69% top-3 accuracy among 26 classes. Its limitations are a single home and only 60 labelled days. Possible extensions are sequence models (GRU/Transformer) over episodes, semi-supervised use of the 10 unlabelled months, and predicting when the next activity will start.
